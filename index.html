Profit First, plain English

Take profit off the top. Run the business on what's left.

Most businesses use the formula Sales minus Expenses equals Profit, and end up with nothing left over. Profit First flips it: Sales minus Profit equals Expenses. You divide every deposit into separate accounts before you spend a dollar.

This calculator gives you starting percentages based on your situation. You'll re-tune them every 90 days as the business grows. There's no perfect number on day one. The discipline is the point.

About your business

A few quick basics.

This shifts how much money goes to your paycheck vs. profit and runway.

Full-time: The business pays your bills. Owner's Pay needs to be substantial.

Side hustle: A day job covers your life. The business can route more money into Profit, gear, and growth instead of paychecks.

This shapes how taxes flow.

Sole Prop / single-member LLC: Profits flow to your personal taxes. The Tax account funds your quarterly estimateds.

S-Corp: You're on payroll. Tax is withheld from your paycheck, but you still keep a Tax account so the business can reimburse you for personal estimated payments and avoid the "feels like a pay cut" trap.

Either way, the 15% Tax allocation stays.

Best guess. The official Profit First targets shift as a business gets bigger.

As revenue grows, Owner's Pay shrinks (you can hire help), and Profit grows. The official targets:

  • $0 to $250K: Profit 5%, Owner 50%, Tax 15%, OpEx 30%
  • $250K to $500K: Profit 10%, Owner 35%, Tax 15%, OpEx 40%
  • $500K+: Profit 15%, Owner 20%, Tax 15%, OpEx 50%

How to actually run it

  1. 1
    Open 5 separate accounts

    Income, Profit, Owner's Comp, Tax, OpEx. Use Relay (free, 20 accounts, official Profit First partner) or a credit union with no per-account fees.

  2. 2
    All deposits land in Income

    Don't spend a dollar from Income. It's a holding account.

  3. 3
    On the 10th & 25th, distribute

    Move Income's full balance into the four buckets using your percentages. Pay all bills only from OpEx.

  4. 4

  5. 5
    Re-tune every 90 days

    Bump Profit up 1 to 2 points each quarter. Trim OpEx by the same amount. The discipline forces efficiency.

  6. !
    If OpEx runs dry: cut costs, don't raid

    When OpEx empties before the next allocation, you do NOT pull from Profit, Tax, or Owner's Pay. You delay, renegotiate, or kill the expense. That's the system working.
