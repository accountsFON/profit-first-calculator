Profit First, in Plain English

Take profit off the top.
Run the business on what's left.

Most small businesses use the formula Sales minus Expenses equals Profit, and end up with no profit at all. That's not a math problem. That's a behavior problem.

Profit First flips the formula: Sales minus Profit equals Expenses. Every deposit gets sliced into separate accounts before you spend a dollar. Profit, owner's pay, and taxes get protected first. What's left is what runs the business.

The Big Idea: Small Plates

When you eat off a giant platter, you fill the platter. When you eat off a small plate, your portion looks normal and you eat less. Same food, different container.

One giant operating account is a giant platter. Profit First gives your business smaller plates. The money for profit, taxes, and your paycheck is physically out of reach, so the business runs lean by design instead of by willpower.

Full-time: The business has to pay your bills. Owner's Comp needs to be substantial, around 50% of revenue when you're starting out.

Side hustle: A day job covers your life. The business can route more money into Profit, gear, and growth instead of paychecks. We'll bump Profit up and shrink Owner's Comp.

Either way, you should still pay yourself. The Owner's Comp account exists even if you "don't need the money" right now. Habits compound.

This shapes how taxes flow through to you.

Sole Prop / single-member LLC: Profits flow to your personal taxes. The Tax account funds your quarterly estimated payments to the IRS.

S-Corp: You're on payroll, taxes withheld from your paycheck. You still keep a Tax account so the business reimburses you for personal estimateds. If you don't, withholding feels like a pay cut and you'll resent the system.

Bottom line: the 15% Tax allocation stays no matter the entity. The behavior matters more than the bookkeeping mechanics.

Best guess. The official Profit First targets shift as a business gets bigger.

As revenue grows, Owner's Comp shrinks (you can afford to hire and pay employees from OpEx) and Profit grows. The official targets:

  • $0 to $250K: Profit 5%, Owner 50%, Tax 15%, OpEx 30%
  • $250K to $500K: Profit 10%, Owner 35%, Tax 15%, OpEx 40%
  • $500K to $1M: Profit 15%, Owner 20%, Tax 15%, OpEx 50%
  • $1M+: Profit 10%, Owner 10%, Tax 15%, OpEx 65%

Why Owner's Comp drops: at higher revenue, the business pays a team out of OpEx instead of relying on you alone. You take less salary but a bigger piece of profit.

2. What Each Account Is For

Income

A holding tank. Every deposit lands here first. You never spend from this account. It's only purpose is to accumulate money between allocation days.

Profit

Your reward for taking risk. Hands off until quarterly distribution day. This is not for reinvestment, not for "I'll use it next month if I'm short," not for emergencies (that's what the Vault is for). It's profit. Treat it like it doesn't exist between distributions.

Owner's Comp

Your paycheck. Pay yourself a predictable amount on a predictable schedule, like a real job. If you're an LLC/sole prop, take draws on the 10th and 25th. If you're an S-corp, fund payroll from this account.

Tax

Reserved for the IRS, state, and self-employment tax. Covers both the business's taxes and your personal estimated payments. Even if you're on payroll with withholding, you keep this account so the business reimburses you. Otherwise withholding feels like a pay cut and the system fails behaviorally.

Operating Expenses

Everything else: software, gear, ads, payroll, contractors, supplies, rent. If this runs dry before the next allocation, you DO NOT raid the other accounts. You delay, renegotiate, or cut. That is the system telling you costs are too high.

3. Set Up the Accounts

Use a bank with no per-account fees and no minimums. Profit First's official banking partner is Relay (free, 20 accounts, percentage-based auto-transfers, ~10 minute setup). A local credit union also works.

Bank 1 (operations, daily access)

  • •Income
  • •Owner's Comp (linked to your personal account for transfers)
  • •Operating Expenses (this is the only one with a debit card)

Bank 2 (out of sight, no debit card)

  • •Profit
  • •Tax

Why two banks? Temptation removal. Money you can see and easily move to OpEx will get moved to OpEx. A different bank with a 1-2 day transfer delay creates just enough friction to keep your hands off Profit and Tax. Many people use a separate online savings account or credit union for Bank 2.

4. The 10/25 Rhythm

Twice a month, on the 10th and 25th, you do two things:

  1. Take whatever's in Income and split it across the four other accounts using your percentages.
  2. Pay all bills due only from OpEx.

Why these dates? Bills land at vendors by the 15th and end of month, which lines up. Twice-monthly batching is more efficient than ad-hoc transfers, and it lets you start to see cash flow waves: when income piles up vs. drains, you spot trends.

Rule of thumb: don't go more frequent than weekly, and don't allocate sporadically. The pattern is the point.

5. Quarterly Distributions

On the last business day of March, June, September, and December:

Take 50% of your Profit account balance as your owner bonus. The other 50% stays as a rainy-day cushion.

This is the reward for taking the risk of running a business. It is not for reinvestment, not for paying down debt, not for buying gear. It's profit. Spend it on yourself or save it. Anything else defeats the system.

Debt-snowball mode: 95% of your Profit balance attacks your smallest debt as a lump-sum payment. The remaining 5% is a small reward to keep the habit alive.

Why smallest debt first instead of highest interest? Behavioral momentum. Small wins compound into the consistency you need to keep going. Once all debt is gone, switch back to the standard 50/50 owner-bonus rule.

6. The Rules That Make It Stick

  • 01 No raiding. When OpEx runs dry, you cut, delay, or renegotiate. You do not pull from Profit, Tax, or Owner's Comp. That's the system working as designed.
  • 02 No peeking at Bank 2. Profit and Tax accounts are out of sight on purpose. Don't log in just to check the balance. The temptation builds with familiarity.
  • 03 Re-tune every 90 days. Bump Profit up 1 to 2 points each quarter. Trim OpEx by the same. The discipline forces efficiency.
  • 04 Don't run up expenses to cut taxes. Spending $10 to "save" $3 in taxes is a $7 loss. Run the business as profitably as possible and pay the tax.
  • 05 Start now even if it's 1%. Don't wait until you're "profitable enough." The system forces profitability. Starting at 1% and ramping is dramatically better than waiting.
  • 06 Don't skip Tax just because you're on payroll. Withholding feels like a pay cut. The Tax account is what reimburses you, restoring the felt income. Keep the account no matter your entity type.

7. Your First-Week Checklist

  1. 1 Open 5 accounts (Income, Profit, Owner's Comp, Tax, OpEx). Use Relay or a credit union.
  2. 2 Move Profit and Tax to a second bank (or separate online savings) with no debit card.
  3. 3 Update payment processors (Stripe, ACH, etc.) to deposit into Income, not your old operating account.
  4. 4 Set calendar reminders for the 10th and 25th of every month, plus the last business day of each quarter.
  5. 5 Run your first allocation on the next 10th or 25th, whichever comes first. Don't backfill historical money. Start fresh.
  6. 6 Re-run this calculator in 90 days and bump Profit up 1 to 2 points.
